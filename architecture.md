High-Level Architecture

Quvvatla is being developed as a modular mobile EV charging service designed to complement existing charging infrastructure in Uzbekistan.

The architecture is designed around customer convenience and time efficiency, enabling charging to fit more naturally into users’ daily routines rather than requiring users to organize their time around fixed charging locations.

This document presents only the high-level architecture of the project. Detailed technical implementation, proprietary specifications, commercial logic, supplier information, and operating procedures are intentionally excluded from this public repository.

1. Energy Delivery Layer

The solution includes a mobile energy delivery component capable of supporting EV charging operations at locations where fixed charging infrastructure may be unavailable, inconvenient, occupied, or insufficient for the customer’s needs.

The detailed energy configuration, electrical architecture, capacity parameters, and component specifications are confidential.

2. Charging Interface Layer

The charging interface connects the mobile energy solution with electric vehicles and supports controlled charging operations.

The project is being designed to allow compatibility with relevant EV charging requirements and future pilot use cases.

Detailed charging hardware specifications and integration parameters are not disclosed publicly.

3. Service Operations Layer

The operational layer supports the delivery of mobile charging as a service.

It is designed not only to coordinate charging operations, but also to minimize the amount of time and effort required from the customer.

At a high level, this may include:

receiving charging requests;
coordinating service availability;
scheduling and dispatching charging operations;
completing charging sessions;
monitoring service performance;
collecting customer and operational feedback.

Detailed operational workflows, dispatch logic, and internal procedures are confidential.

4. Digital Support Layer

Future digital tools may support the coordination and management of Quvvatla services.

Potential functions may include:

customer requests;
service scheduling;
charging session records;
operational monitoring;
service coordination;
pilot data collection;
customer communication.

The objective of the digital layer is to make the charging process as simple and low-effort as possible for the customer.

Software development is planned as part of the future MVP stage.

5. Pilot and Validation Layer

The initial architecture is designed to support a controlled pilot in Tashkent.

The pilot will be used to validate:

technical feasibility;
customer demand;
customer convenience;
time-saving value;
service accessibility;
operational requirements;
response times;
service reliability;
user experience;
scalability potential.

6. Modular Development Approach

Quvvatla is being designed with a modular development approach so that individual components of the service can be tested, validated, and improved during the MVP and pilot stages.

This approach is intended to support future adaptation to different customer segments and operational scenarios without requiring the full future system to be developed at the initial stage.

Confidentiality Boundary

The following information is not included in this public repository:

detailed energy architecture;
electrical system specifications;
charging equipment specifications;
supplier and technology partner information;
proprietary integration design;
routing and dispatch logic;
pricing and commercial models;
unit economics;
detailed operational procedures;
implementation algorithms.

These elements remain confidential and will be developed and documented separately.
