# Development Roadmap

Quvvatla is an early-stage EnergyTech startup developing a service-based mobile EV charging solution for Uzbekistan.

The project is built around one central principle:

**EV charging should adapt to people's lives — not the other way around.**

Quvvatla's development roadmap is milestone-driven. Each stage is intended to validate critical assumptions, reduce technical, market, operational, regulatory, and financial uncertainty, and create a stronger foundation for the next stage of development.

---

## Stage 1 — Pre-MVP

**Current stage**

The current stage is focused on validating the concept, preparing the future MVP and pilot, building strategic relationships, securing the resources required for implementation, and reducing the main technical, market, operational, financial, and regulatory uncertainties.

Quvvatla has already established initial dialogue with several potential technology and strategic partners.

Current discussions include potential cooperation models, suitable technology configurations, equipment options, and conditions for future pilot deployment.

### Key Workstreams

* market and customer problem validation;
* technology partner search and engagement;
* strategic partnership development;
* evaluation of potential cooperation models;
* evaluation of suitable technology and equipment options;
* technical feasibility assessment;
* service architecture development;
* initial business model validation;
* regulatory assessment;
* MVP scope definition;
* pilot design and planning;
* grant and non-dilutive funding applications;
* investor and other funding opportunity exploration;
* international accelerator and innovation programme applications;
* exploration of Horizon Europe funding opportunities, relevant calls, and potential consortium participation.

### Stage Outcome

The Pre-MVP stage is intended to establish:

* a validated problem and service concept;
* a defined MVP scope;
* a viable pilot design;
* suitable technology and strategic partnership options;
* a clearer regulatory pathway;
* a funding strategy for MVP and pilot implementation;
* sufficient evidence to proceed toward MVP development.

---

## Stage 2 — MVP Development and Operational Preparation

The MVP stage will focus on creating the minimum operational version of Quvvatla required for real-world testing.

The objective is not simply to demonstrate that mobile charging is technically possible.

The MVP must be capable of testing whether Quvvatla can deliver a convenient, reliable, time-efficient, and commercially viable service under real conditions.

### Key Workstreams

* finalization of key technology and strategic partnerships;
* selection and configuration of the technology solution;
* legal establishment of the operating company;
* development of the initial website;
* development of basic application and digital service functionality;
* customer request and order-management design;
* scheduling and dispatch process development;
* tariff and time-slot configuration;
* operational workflow development;
* internal cost-tracking framework;
* recruitment of key operational personnel;
* preparation of staff training;
* technical support process design;
* development of pilot monitoring and data-collection systems.

### Stage Outcome

The stage is expected to result in:

* an operational MVP ready for controlled testing;
* the initial digital service layer;
* defined customer and operational workflows;
* an established legal and organizational structure;
* trained core personnel;
* validated technical readiness for pilot deployment.

* identification and preparation of a suitable operational base and supporting infrastructure;
* assessment of locations capable of supporting efficient service coverage;
* preparation of technical and operational support arrangements;
* engagement with relevant public authorities and regulatory stakeholders;
* clarification of licensing, permitting, safety, technical, and operational requirements;

Quvvatla should enter the pilot with:

* operational infrastructure in place;
* regulatory and legal requirements sufficiently clarified;
* trained personnel;
* functional digital tools;
* tested internal workflows;
* pilot customers ready for controlled deployment.

## Stage 4 — Controlled Pilot

The first controlled pilot is planned for Tashkent, Uzbekistan.

The pilot will test the complete Quvvatla service model under real urban conditions.

The objective is to determine whether the combination of customer demand, technical performance, logistics, digital coordination, team execution, pricing, and operating economics can support a viable mobile EV charging service.

### Core Validation Areas

#### Market Demand

The pilot will assess:

* real charging demand;
* priority customer segments;
* demand by geographic area;
* demand by day and time;
* scheduled versus urgent demand;
* preferred tariffs and charging time slots;
* repeat usage;
* unmet demand caused by limited service capacity.

#### Customer Value

The pilot will test whether Quvvatla delivers its core value proposition:

* reduced customer time spent managing charging;
* greater convenience;
* greater flexibility;
* better integration of charging into customers' daily routines;
* willingness to use the service repeatedly.

Actual customer behavior and repeat usage will be considered more important than positive feedback alone.

#### Logistics and Service Delivery

The pilot will evaluate:

* order-processing time;
* dispatch performance;
* route and schedule execution;
* ETA accuracy;
* real travel times;
* performance during peak traffic;
* impact of road closures, road works, temporary restrictions, and unexpected congestion;
* ability to recover the schedule after delays;
* number of customer orders that can realistically be completed per shift.

#### Mobile Unit Capacity

The pilot will measure:

* real operating capacity;
* number of charging sessions possible before recharging is required;
* resources required between customer orders;
* impact of customer locations on daily capacity;
* downtime and utilization.

#### Mobile Unit Recharging

The pilot will evaluate:

* actual recharge time;
* partial versus full recharge scenarios;
* frequency of recharging;
* optimal recharge periods;
* effect of recharging on daily service capacity and availability.

#### Digital System Performance

The pilot will validate key digital processes including:

* customer requests;
* order registration;
* location handling;
* scheduling;
* dispatch;
* ETA calculation;
* tariff calculation;
* time-slot management;
* customer communication;
* rescheduling;
* cancellation handling;
* operational monitoring.

#### Technical Performance

The pilot will evaluate:

* successful and failed charging sessions;
* charging reliability;
* service interruptions;
* technical incidents;
* downtime;
* compatibility and operational performance.

#### Team and Process Performance

The pilot will assess:

* staff coordination;
* workload;
* process bottlenecks;
* internal communication;
* operational hand-offs;
* technical support;
* level of manual intervention required.

#### Commercial and Unit Economics Validation

Internally, Quvvatla will compare planned assumptions with real pilot economics, including:

* actual operating cost per completed order;
* travel-related costs;
* energy and service costs;
* staffing and operating costs;
* service utilization;
* revenue by service type;
* tariff performance;
* contribution economics;
* impact of distance and traffic on profitability;
* minimum utilization required for economic viability.

Detailed commercial calculations will remain confidential.

### Data and Operational Learning

Pilot data will also help Quvvatla understand the relationship between:

**location → travel time → service duration → capacity → cost → tariff → customer demand.**

Demand patterns may be analyzed across geography, customer segments, tariffs, preferred charging times, and service intensity.

Over time, this operational data can support better service planning, capacity allocation, scheduling, and future network development.

### Pilot Decision Gate

Pilot results will support a structured:

**GO / ADJUST / REASSESS**

decision before broader deployment.

## Stage 5 — Validation and Model Adjustment

Pilot results will be converted into clear product, operational, and commercial decisions.

### Key Areas of Adjustment

* priority customer segments;
* service areas;
* operating hours;
* demand peaks;
* tariff structure;
* charging time-slot strategy;
* scheduled versus priority service;
* fleet and mobile-unit capacity requirements;
* recharge strategy;
* logistics and dispatch processes;
* scheduling logic;
* digital functionality;
* staffing model;
* customer communication;
* technical configuration;
* cost structure;
* utilization targets;
* unit economics.

### Decision Framework

**GO** — proceed if the pilot demonstrates meaningful demand, reliable operations, clear customer value, manageable logistics, and a credible path to sustainable economics.

**ADJUST** — continue development while modifying operational, technical, pricing, scheduling, or commercial elements.

**REASSESS** — reconsider the current model if the pilot demonstrates insufficient demand, major technical limitations, unacceptable operating complexity, or economics that cannot reasonably support scale.

### Stage Outcome

A validated and refined operating model ready for commercial-readiness preparation.

## Stage 7 — Commercial Launch

The initial commercial launch will focus on customer segments, locations, and operating periods where pilot evidence demonstrates the strongest combination of demand, customer value, operational feasibility, and attractive economics.

Quvvatla will avoid unnecessary geographic expansion before the core operating model is proven.

### Key Performance Areas

* demand;
* repeat usage;
* customer retention;
* service reliability;
* response time;
* ETA accuracy;
* customer time-saving value;
* mobile-unit utilization;
* operating efficiency;
* tariff performance;
* time-slot demand;
* cost per order;
* unit economics;
* service profitability;
* customer-support performance.

Commercial parameters may continue to be adjusted based on real market behavior.

---

## Stage 8 — Scale and Network Development

Once the commercial model has been validated, Quvvatla may expand through controlled increases in service capacity and network coverage.

Potential development areas include:

* additional mobile charging capacity;
* expansion into high-demand service zones;
* corporate fleet relationships;
* logistics and transportation partnerships;
* commercial property and parking partnerships;
* improved scheduling and dispatch capabilities;
* enhanced digital functionality;
* operational automation;
* stronger data analytics;
* additional service formats;
* strategic technology partnerships.

### Expansion Principle

Expansion decisions will be based on evidence including:

* demand density;
* geographic demand clusters;
* peak-load patterns;
* service utilization;
* response-time performance;
* customer retention;
* operating economics;
* profitability;
* supporting infrastructure availability.

The objective is to scale capacity without sacrificing customer experience, reliability, or financial discipline.

---

## Stage 9 — Future Regional Development

After establishing a validated and commercially sustainable operating model in Uzbekistan, Quvvatla may evaluate opportunities in other markets with similar EV charging and urban-mobility needs.

Regional development will depend on:

* EV adoption;
* charging infrastructure conditions;
* customer behavior;
* urban mobility patterns;
* regulatory requirements;
* technology partnerships;
* operating economics;
* availability of local strategic partners;
* scalability of the Quvvatla service model.

Regional expansion will only be considered after the core model has been validated in the initial market.

---

## Funding and Capital Strategy

Quvvatla's funding approach will evolve together with the project.

Early development will prioritize grants, non-dilutive funding, innovation programmes, strategic partnerships, and other suitable sources of pilot financing.

Following successful pilot validation, the project may evaluate additional capital required for commercial deployment, capacity expansion, digital development, and future network growth.

Funding decisions will be tied to validated milestones rather than expansion assumptions alone.

---

## Roadmap Principle

Quvvatla's development will be evidence-driven and milestone-based.

Each stage is designed to reduce uncertainty before the next major technical, financial, operational, or expansion commitment.

The objective is not simply to deploy mobile charging capacity.

The objective is to build a reliable service that gives EV owners greater freedom over their time by reducing the effort required to manage vehicle charging.

**EV charging should adapt to people's lives — not the other way around.**

---

## Confidentiality

This public roadmap intentionally does not disclose:

* detailed technology architecture;
* energy-system specifications;
* equipment specifications;
* supplier and technology-partner information;
* detailed commercial terms;
* actual pricing;
* actual unit economics;
* proprietary scheduling logic;
* dispatch algorithms;
* internal operating procedures;
* detailed regulatory strategy;
* proprietary data methods;
* implementation details;
* other proprietary commercial or operational know-how.

These elements remain confidential and will be developed, tested, and documented separately as Quvvatla progresses from Pre-MVP through pilot, commercial launch, and future scale.
