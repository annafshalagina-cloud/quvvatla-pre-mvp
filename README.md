# Quvvatla

Quvvatla is an early-stage EnergyTech startup developing a service-based mobile EV charging solution for Uzbekistan.

The project is built around one central principle:

**EV charging should adapt to people's lives — not the other way around.**

## Project Status

**Pre-MVP**

Quvvatla is currently at the concept development and Pre-MVP stage.

This repository provides a high-level overview of the project concept, proposed solution, product architecture, MVP scope, pilot approach, and development roadmap.

Software development is planned as part of the future MVP development stage and is not yet included in this repository.

Quvvatla has already initiated discussions with potential technology and strategic partners regarding possible cooperation models, suitable technology configurations, equipment options, and future pilot deployment.

The project is also actively exploring grant, non-dilutive funding, international innovation programmes, strategic partnerships, and other suitable financing opportunities required to progress toward MVP and pilot implementation.

## Problem

The growth of electric vehicle adoption is increasing demand for accessible, reliable, and flexible charging infrastructure.

However, the charging experience can also require EV owners to spend personal time:

* searching for available charging stations;
* travelling to charging locations;
* checking availability;
* waiting for an available charging slot;
* organizing their schedules around the charging process.

For Quvvatla, the problem is therefore not only access to charging infrastructure.

It is also the amount of **time and effort the customer must spend managing vehicle charging**.

## Solution

Quvvatla is developing a flexible mobile EV charging service designed to complement existing charging infrastructure while making EV charging more convenient, flexible, and time-efficient.

The service concept is designed to bring charging closer to where and when it is needed rather than requiring the customer to organize their day around access to a fixed charging location.

The goal is to reduce the time and effort customers spend managing vehicle charging and allow them to focus more on family, work, health, sport, leisure, and everyday life.

The initial focus is the Uzbekistan market, with potential applications for both private EV users and commercial customers.

## MVP and Pilot Approach

The first stage of development is focused on defining and preparing a practical MVP for real-world testing in Uzbekistan.

The MVP and pilot will be designed to validate the complete service model rather than technical feasibility alone.

Key validation areas will include:

* real customer demand;
* customer convenience and time-saving value;
* priority customer segments;
* logistics and response times;
* scheduling and dispatch performance;
* mobile-unit service capacity;
* charging and operational reliability;
* digital service processes;
* tariff and time-slot demand;
* team and operational workflow;
* service utilization;
* actual operating costs;
* commercial viability and unit-economics assumptions.

The pilot is intended to provide evidence for a structured **GO / ADJUST / REASSESS** decision before broader commercial deployment.

Detailed technical architecture, commercial calculations, operational logic, and implementation specifications are intentionally not disclosed in this public repository.
